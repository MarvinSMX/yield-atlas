The Vanguard High Dividend Yield ETF (VYM) is one of the most widely held dividend ETFs in the United States, with over $90 billion in assets under management. It tracks the FTSE High Dividend Yield Index, which selects U.S. stocks — excluding REITs — forecast to have above-average dividend yields, weighted by market capitalization.

VYM is a cornerstone holding for income investors thanks to its ultra-low 0.06% expense ratio, broad diversification across hundreds of dividend-paying companies, and Vanguard's long track record of delivering consistent quarterly income. Since its inception in 2006, VYM has paid dividends every single year.

Its quarterly distribution schedule — typically in March, June, September, and December — gives investors predictable, four-times-per-year income from a portfolio of financially strong U.S. companies across every major sector.

How to Use the VYM Dividend Schedule

Three key dates determine whether you qualify for a VYM dividend and when you get paid.

Understanding the Three Key Dates

  • Ex-Dividend Date: You must own VYM shares before this date to receive the dividend. Buying on or after the ex-date means you miss that quarter's payment.
  • Record Date: The date Vanguard officially records eligible shareholders. For VYM, the ex-dividend date and record date are typically the same day each quarter.
  • Payable Date: The date the dividend is deposited into your brokerage account — typically 3–5 business days after the ex-dividend date.
  1. Find the upcoming VYM ex-dividend date Use the table above. You must hold shares before this date to qualify for that quarter's payment.
  2. Purchase shares before the ex-date Buy VYM at least one trading day before the ex-dividend date so your trade settles in time.
  3. Hold through the record date Stay a shareholder on the record date (same day as ex-date for VYM).
  4. Receive your payment on the payable date Your VYM dividend is credited to your brokerage account approximately 3–5 days after the ex-date.

💡 Dividend Reinvestment (DRIP)

Most brokerages allow you to automatically reinvest your VYM dividends back into additional shares via a Dividend Reinvestment Plan (DRIP). Because VYM holds hundreds of large-cap dividend-paying companies, reinvesting your quarterly distributions compounds both the income stream and the underlying equity growth over time.
