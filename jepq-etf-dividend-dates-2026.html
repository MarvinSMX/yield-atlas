When Does JEPQ Pay Its Dividend in 2026?

The JPMorgan Nasdaq Equity Premium Income ETF (JEPQ) distributes monthly. The ex-dividend date is normally the first business day of the month, with the record date falling on the same day and the pay date roughly two business days later.

JEPQ's September 2026 distribution came in at $0.68255 per share, with an ex-date of September 1 and a pay date of September 3. That is a 3.2% step down from August's record $0.70497, but it still ranks as the second-largest monthly payout in the fund's history, edging past the $0.68125 from November 2022 that had stood as the all-time high until this August.

Across 2026 so far, JEPQ has paid $4.71 per share over eight distributions, an average of $0.5891 per month, well ahead of 2025's $0.5102 monthly average and on pace for the fund's strongest year since inception. Over the trailing twelve months JEPQ has distributed $6.76 per share, a yield of roughly 11%.

One quirk catches investors out every year: JEPQ has no January ex-dividend date. The explanation is below.

⚠️ Why JEPQ Has No January Ex-Dividend Date

Look at any JEPQ calendar and January appears to be missing. It isn't — JEPQ runs two December ex-dividend dates every year. One falls at the start of December as normal, and a second falls in the final days of the month. That late-December distribution has a pay date in early January, which is the payment shareholders receive as their January income.

This has happened every single year since inception:

  • Ex Dec 29, 2022 → paid Jan 4, 2023
  • Ex Dec 28, 2023 → paid Jan 3, 2024
  • Ex Dec 31, 2024 → paid Jan 3, 2025
  • Ex Dec 31, 2025 → paid Jan 5, 2026 ($0.5761)

The practical consequence: if you buy JEPQ in early January expecting a January ex-date, you have already missed it. The relevant ex-date was in late December. Expect the same structure at the end of 2026.

Why JEPQ's Dividend Changes Every Month

JEPQ's payout is variable by design. Understanding why prevents unnecessary panic when it drops.

How JEPQ Generates Its Income

JEPQ holds an actively managed portfolio of Nasdaq-100 stocks and layers on an options overlay that sells call exposure on the index. The premiums collected from those options, plus dividends from the underlying holdings, fund the monthly distribution.

Option premiums scale with implied volatility. When markets get choppy, calls sell for more and JEPQ's payout rises. When markets are calm and grinding upward, premiums shrink and the payout falls. This is why the distribution swings between roughly $0.34 and $0.70 rather than holding steady.

A falling JEPQ distribution is not a dividend cut in the way a company reducing its payout is. It reflects a lower-volatility month. The trade-off runs the other way too: the record August 2026 payout came out of a higher-volatility stretch in tech.

💡 How to Qualify for a JEPQ Distribution

  • Buy before the ex-dividend date. Purchase no later than the prior trading day. Buying on the ex-date itself means you miss that month.
  • Ex-date and record date are the same day for JEPQ, so there is no separate window to track.
  • You can sell on or after the ex-date and still receive the payment. Ownership before the ex-date is what matters.
  • The pay date lands about two business days later — faster than most monthly ETFs, which often run 5+ days.
